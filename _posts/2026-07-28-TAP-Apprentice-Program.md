**Respected Guests, Dignitaries, fellow partners, teachers, and students, good morning to you all.**

After my basic info...

My personal beliefs are:

1. Every individual has **potential**.
2. It is always a **choice** for each individual to pick *only good things* from the environment.
3. Deep down, I believe in the **Advaita philosophy**: *all in one & one in all.*

I will be talking on the **"Role of technology in education shaping the future of Bharat."**

Technology is a breakthrough that is initially in very few hands, and
eventually, it spreads to the masses. **Every time it reaches the masses,
disruption happens**—e.g., *the printing press, radio, TV, internet,
blockchain,* and now **AI**.

My friend and colleague from IIT Bombay, *Dr. Ravi G*, was on a mission to
search for the **next Ramanujan**. He believed this person would come from a
rural village, so he started going to rural villages in Karnataka, giving logic
tests to students, and mentoring the ones who performed well. But then COVID
hit, and they had to move to screens. Ravi, who was *very skeptical* about
online education, welcomed it and went online. **That move helped him expand his
program to a much bigger base.** This was all possible because of the spread of
the internet and handheld devices, *even in remote villages*.

My personal experience comes from a couple of courses I took online. They helped
me break the daily routine and learn new things that were very useful in the
future. E.g., coming from an engineering background, I always felt the gap of
not knowing the foundations of finance. **One of the courses I did almost 20
years ago helped me pivot into the fintech domain.**

Recently, I finished learning basic **Sanskrit** online and enrolled into
advanced one now. This happened because the classes were in the late evening
when working professionals like me could join, and exams were conducted online
with emotion, gesture, and screen monitoring so people couldn't cheat.

*As they say, always learning new things keeps you in the game.*

Just as there is a bright side to technology in education, **there is a darker
side too.**

These devices & apps are *addictive* and co-exist with other apps designed to
seek attention from users. It's not just kids—*parents and elders also fall
victim to it.* **Unlike physical harm where you see smoke or blood, digital harm
is invisible.** By the time we notice the damage, the addiction is already deep,
and it is difficult to identify early. I know individuals among my extended
family & friends who have been treated for this addiction, and it is tough.

Because power's true nature is to **centralize itself**, a very few *Big Tech*
companies are controlling the entire show; *it's like a monopoly.* The model is
very clear: it begins free of cost to accumulate data and identify patterns from
users, and then algorithms keep targeting individuals for subscriptions, going
even to the level of **manipulating thoughts** and controlling individuals or
groups.

There could be multiple solutions to a problem, and often there may not be a
one-size-fits-all answer. E.g., using & teaching only open-source free software
like **GNU/Linux** in schools could be one approach, where by its license, *it
is open to study, modify, and share.*

Or **guiding digital boundaries** and monitoring the usage of handheld devices
for your kids & family *until they develop the maturity to navigate it safely.*

**AI is the biggest elephant in the room now.** Its challenges are even harder,
and the solutions will be harder still.

However, technology keeps changing, and we should be preparing the new
generation of Bharat for the problems of tomorrow that we have not yet seen.
**Technology can give our youth skills, but only character-building education
will give them wisdom.**

I would love to end my talk with the words of **Swami Vivekananda**, which I
feel are even more relevant in today's world of AI:

> *"Education is not the amount of information that is put into your brain and
> runs riot there, undigested, all your life.
>
> We must have life-building, > man-making, character-making
> assimilation of ideas."*

**Thank you!**
