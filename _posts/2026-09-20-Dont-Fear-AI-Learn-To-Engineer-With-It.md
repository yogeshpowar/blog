> If you are a student in Computer Science, IT or Engineering, don't let fear
> of AI define how you learn. AI changes the tools of engineering - it does
> not remove the need for judgment, understanding, and ownership. Don't
> compete with AI at being a faster typist. Learn to become a better engineer
> with AI as your tool.

If you are a student or a young aspirant in Computer Science, IT or
Engineering, you will hear a lot about AI taking away jobs.

Don't let that fear define how you learn.

AI will certainly change the nature of software work. Some tasks will become
automated, and some roles will evolve. But that does not mean the end of
engineering. It means the tools of engineering are becoming more powerful.

Think of AI as a tool that can help you:

- explore a new concept from different perspectives,
- learn at a pace that suits your own ability to grasp it,
- explore multiple approaches to a problem,
- write, read and reason about code more efficiently,
- debug and test your ideas,
- navigate unfamiliar technologies and codebases,
- experiment more freely,
- and spend more time thinking about what should be built and why.

But the tool does not do the learning for you.

Your ability to understand, question, connect ideas and develop judgment
remains your own. AI can explain something differently, give you examples,
challenge your thinking or help you explore - but whether you actually
understand it depends on you.

The important skill is therefore not merely knowing how to write code.

It is understanding problems deeply, thinking clearly, designing systems,
questioning assumptions, and taking responsibility for the solution.

The pessimistic view of AI is largely a question for technology leaders,
businesses and governments: what happens when machines can do more of the
work?

For students, there is a more useful question:

What can I learn and build when I have these powerful tools alongside me?

Don't compete with AI at being a faster typist.

Learn to become a better engineer with AI as your tool.

The future will need people who can combine human curiosity, judgment and
responsibility with increasingly powerful tools to solve real problems.
